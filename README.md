# Out Of Character — Game Description Overview

## What This Is

**Out Of Character** is a Questner Game Description (GD) for a high-difficulty AI-driven fantasy roleplaying game.

The central idea is simple:

> The player must not only make good decisions. They must make decisions that fit their character.

The game is run by an AI dungeon master named Mandy. The player chooses a class, a name, a personality strength, and a personality flaw. From that point onward, their actions are judged not only by tactical quality, but also by whether they remain true to the character they created.

If the player acts in character, the world may still be dangerous. If the player acts out of character, the world becomes hostile.

The result is intended to feel like a roleplaying challenge, not just a fantasy adventure.

## Origin of the Game Description

This GD began as a revision of a Questner community game called **In Character**.

The original concept was already strong: a fantasy RPG where the player is scored on how well they act according to their character's personality and class. The original game used an **In Character Score** from 1 to 5, with low scores making the world more hostile.

After extensive playtesting, several strengths and weaknesses became clear.

The strengths were:

* The core roleplaying challenge was fun.
* The In Character Score created meaningful pressure.
* The AI game master was good at interpreting dice rolls.
* The game was capable of maintaining surprisingly rich story memory over long play.

The weaknesses were:

* The original In Character Score mechanic was strong, but the meaning of each score level needed clearer definitions.
* The original GD strongly emphasized punishment for low In Character Scores, but it did not clearly define how different score levels should translate into different levels of hostility.
* The narrator often moved the story forward too quickly.
* The world sometimes escalated immediately after every battle or quest.
* There was no explicit way for the definition of "in character" to evolve over time.
* The original realm had become familiar after extensive play.

**Out Of Character** was created as a new version of that idea: the same core challenge, but with clearer design rules, a new setting, better pacing, and support for character development.

## Core Design Philosophy

### 1. Good-Faith Play

This GD assumes the player is acting in good faith.

It is not designed as an anti-cheat system. It does not try to aggressively prevent the player from forcing outcomes, inventing items, or exploiting the AI. If a player wants to cheat, that is their business.

Instead, the GD focuses on making the game enjoyable for a player who wants to roleplay honestly.

This philosophy is important because too much defensive prompting can make an AI roleplaying game feel suspicious, adversarial, or mechanical. **Out Of Character** is strict, but it is not paranoid.

### 2. Strict but Fair Consequences

The world should punish the player for acting out of character, but the punishment should feel like a logical consequence.

The game should not be randomly cruel.

A low In Character Score can cause suspicion, hostility, bad prices, quest complications, ambushes, public shame, guild penalties, or other consequences. But those consequences should connect to what the player actually did wrong.

The goal is not to make the player lose. The goal is to make staying in character matter.

### 3. Roleplay and Tactics Are Separate

One of the major design principles of this GD is that the game should judge three things separately:

1. **Tactical quality** — Was the plan smart, risky, foolish, careful, creative, or reckless?
2. **Roleplay fidelity** — Did the action fit the character's class, strength, flaw, and established personality?
3. **Dice result** — Did luk facvor the player?

These three factors are then combined in the narration.

This means:

* A smart action can still cause problems if it is out of character.
* A foolish action can still be respected if it fits the character.
* A good dice roll should not erase bad roleplay.
* Good roleplay should not guarantee success.
* Luck, tactics, and character fidelity all matter.

### 4. Player Agency Comes First

The narrator should not decide the player's actions or words.

The AI may describe the world, NPCs, consequences, danger, reactions, and opportunities. But it should not take control of the player's character.

This matters especially in a game about acting in character. The player must be allowed to choose how their character behaves, because that choice is the actual game.

### 5. The Game Should Be Dangerous, Not Breathless

The original experience often moved too quickly.

After a battle, the narrator might immediately trigger a cave-in, explosion, ambush, or magical disaster. After a quest ended, the narrator might immediately introduce a dragon, a portal, or an old enemy attacking the city.

Those things can be exciting, but if they happen too often, the player loses room to explore, loot, shop, gossip, rest, or choose their own direction.

**Out Of Character** adds explicit pacing rules:

* After dangerous scenes, the player usually gets breathing room.
* After quests conclude, the game enters downtime mode.
* Downtime should offer grounded options instead of forcing the next crisis.
* Major threats should usually be foreshadowed before becoming immediate.

The world remains dangerous, but it should give the player space to exist inside it.

### 6. Personality Can Evolve

The definition of "in character" should not be frozen forever.

A character may change because of repeated behavior, trauma, relationships, victories, failures, curses, blessings, reputation, or moral growth.

For example:

* A suspicious ranger may slowly learn to trust a companion.
* A proud warrior may become humbler after a major failure.
* A cowardly mage may become braver after surviving real danger.
* A selfish rogue may become protective of a specific NPC.

This is not the same as letting the player instantly redefine their personality whenever convenient. Personality change should feel earned.

To support this, the GD adds the **Current Roleplay Lens**.

## Major Changes from the Original GD

### New Identity

The game was renamed from **In Character** to **Out Of Character**.

The new name emphasizes the core threat of the game: acting out of character is dangerous.

The dungeon master was also renamed from Mindy to Mandy, giving this version its own identity.

### New Realm

The original game took place in Solmara, beginning in the trade city of Lumaria.

**Out Of Character** replaces that with a new northern realm:

* **Kingdom:** Veyrholm
* **Starting city:** Greyhaven
* **Region style:** cold fjords, black cliffs, pine forests, peat bogs, ruined watchtowers, old mines, buried war machines
* **Political background:** Queen Astrid Veyr rules, but her power is weakening
* **Tone:** grim, coastal, cold, political, monster-haunted

This gives the game a fresh atmosphere while keeping the basic adventurer-guild structure intact.

### Clearer In Character Scoring

The original GD used a 1–5 In Character Score, but the meanings of the scores were fairly loose.

**Out Of Character** adds a clearer rubric:

* **5/5:** Strongly in character
* **4/5:** Mostly in character
* **3/5:** Weakly in character or generic
* **2/5:** Clearly out of character
* **1/5:** Severe character break

This should make the scoring more consistent and easier for the player to understand.

### Class Expectations and Taboos

The revised GD requires the narrator to create:

* 3 class expectations
* 2 class taboos

These are created after the player chooses a class.

For example, a ranger might be expected to scout, use terrain, and strike from advantage. A ranger might be punished for rushing blindly into melee or ignoring tracks.

This helps the game judge class-based behavior more consistently.

### Current Roleplay Lens

The **Current Roleplay Lens** is a compact guide for judging whether the player is acting in character.

It tracks:

* Class
* Personality strength
* Personality flaw
* Class expectations
* Class taboos
* Current personality pattern
* Recent roleplay drift

The lens is not a full memory system. It does not override the player's agency, inventory, companions, rank, money, quests, or story memory.

Its purpose is narrower: to keep roleplay scoring consistent, especially during long campaigns where the character may evolve.

### Better Pacing and Downtime

A new **Scene Pacing and Downtime** section was added.

This section tells the narrator not to rush every scene into the next emergency.

During quests, the player should usually get time to loot, inspect, heal, talk, rest briefly, or decide what to do next after a dangerous scene ends.

Between quests, the game should enter downtime mode after a natural conclusion. During downtime, the narrator should not immediately force a new crisis. Instead, the player should be offered grounded options such as:

* visiting the tavern
* exploring the market
* buying or repairing equipment
* visiting the guild board
* training or resting
* speaking with NPCs
* investigating rumors
* choosing a new quest

New hooks are still allowed, but they should usually begin as rumors, signs, letters, requests, dreams, job postings, or warnings before becoming urgent.

### More Explicit Hostility Rules

The original GD said the world should become very hostile when the In Character Score is low.

The revised GD defines what that means:

* **3/5:** hostile pressure
* **2/5:** strong hostility
* **1/5:** harsh consequence

This makes the consequences easier to scale.

A 3/5 should not necessarily cause disaster. It might make NPCs suspicious or create a small complication.

A 1/5 can justify severe consequences such as ambush, arrest, injury, curse, public shame, guild penalty, quest failure, loss of opportunity, or a major NPC turning against the player.

### Preserved Dice System

The dice system was intentionally not heavily changed.

The original game master was already good at interpreting d100 rolls, so this version keeps the same basic approach:

* In uncertain situations, the narrator first outputs only `ROLL_DICE`.
* The dice result is then used in the next response.
* Higher rolls mean better outcomes.
* Lower rolls mean worse outcomes.
* Dice do not determine the In Character Score.
* The In Character Score affects how dice results are interpreted.

### Preserved Good-Faith Memory Rule

The GD keeps the original good-faith memory approach.

If the player refers to an item, companion, event, or promise the AI does not remember, the narrator should assume the player is right and continue.

This is a deliberate choice.

The game is not trying to police the player. It is trying to support smooth roleplay.

## What This GD Is Trying to Achieve

**Out Of Character** is designed to create a specific kind of AI RPG experience:

* high difficulty
* short narration
* strong player agency
* meaningful roleplay pressure
* dangerous but fair consequences
* room for downtime and exploration
* character growth over time
* a world that reacts strongly to personality breaks
* a narrator that respects the player's chosen identity

The ideal player is someone who enjoys being challenged not only tactically, but dramatically.

The game asks:

> Can you survive the adventure while remaining true to who your character is?

## What This GD Is Not Trying to Do

This GD is not designed to be exploit-proof.

It is not designed to prevent all cheating, prompt injection, invented inventory, or forced outcomes.

It is not designed to simulate a complete tabletop ruleset.

It is not designed to turn Questner into a tactical combat engine.

It is also not designed to make the player constantly suffer. The game is harsh, but it should still be fun, readable, and fair.

## Possible Future Improvements

Future versions may explore:

* a clearer rank progression system
* optional quest-board structure
* more detailed downtime activities
* stronger faction mechanics
* alternate realm modules
* separate genre variants
* better guidance for long-term NPC relationships
* examples of good and bad In Character Scores
* optional difficulty modes
* guidance for handling character redemption arcs
* guidance for major personality transformations

These are intentionally left out of v1.0 unless playtesting shows they are needed.

## Design Summary

**Out Of Character** is a roleplaying-first Questner GD built around a simple but powerful rule:

> The player is not only judged by what works. They are judged by what fits.

It keeps the best part of the original game: the pressure to remain in character.

It improves that idea with clearer scoring, better pacing, a new setting, explicit class expectations, fairer hostility rules, and a way for personality to evolve over time.

The intended result is a game that feels strict, reactive, dramatic, and player-driven.
